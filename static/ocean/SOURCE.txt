Jellyfish ocean renderer credits

The current renderer displays two generated photographic jellyfish materials:
assets/images/ocean/moon.png and silver.png. Gold remains an unused earlier study.
These were generated from the owner's shape references; Hugo supplies local WebP variants.
The photographic bells breathe on their own meshes. Curved photographic oral arms
use three separate ribbons on each jellyfish. Their geometry controls coverage
and the source images supply the surface detail. Another 28 translucent
filaments are independently modeled, each with its own frequency, phase, amplitude
and traveling root-to-tip bend. The long tails no longer share a deforming sheet.
Attachment points are sampled inside opaque tissue in each local texture and use
the same bell deformation as the cap. During turns, successive tail sections lag
behind the head using recent poses; the individual strand rhythms remain separate.

The cohesive seabed scene and red king crab carapace are also original generated
assets for this site. Rocks, shells, starfish and seagrass share one perspective
and underwater light. The king crab has six visible walking legs and two unequal
claws; a curved frontal shell surface and projected joints follow a two-dimensional
seabed route with planted feet, diagonal motion, pauses and changes of direction.
Morphology reference: https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/red-king-crab

These photographs are resized as local WebP images and sampled with adjusted
exposure, color and projection. The previous generated skin is no longer used.
The five owner-supplied photographs guided the head and fin geometry. The
closed-mouth underwater views guided the melon, upper lip and lower jaw.

https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Beluga03.jpg

Anatomy reference: https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/beluga-whale
The three-dimensional body has paired pectoral fins and horizontal tail flukes.
Swimming alternates strokes and glides. The neck leads a turn, the inside
flipper draws in, and the body follows the recent swim path. The tail bends and
its tips flex on each stroke.
Turn and flipper reference: Frank E. Fish, Balancing Requirements for Stability
and Maneuverability in Cetaceans. https://doi.org/10.1093/icb/42.1.85
Motion reference: 白鲸游泳姿态展示, 关关在立夏
https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1Fq4y1o7im/
